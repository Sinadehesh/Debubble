Debubble Room

Your room mirrors your real body, room and heart. When they need care it gets messy; doing the real task cleans it, and earns something for the room. You start with nothing. This is the app's engine running in the browser: tap a task, skip ahead a few days, drag things around.

Sealed room

Drag anything, including yourself. Floor things stay on the floor, wall things on the wall. Tap to select.

Day 1

"Do a month" plays thirty days of the three care tasks at tiny or standard size and one mission a day, so you can see the room fill up and the walls open.

Habits need sameness

Automaticity comes from repeating the same act at the same moment (Lally et al., 2010; Wood & Rünger, 2016). So each care area has one routine you keep, at one cue. What changes day to day is its size, never the act.

Tiny counts in full

A two-minute version earns the day as fully as a long one, because repetition is what builds the habit (Fogg; Clear). Size only changes how clean the room gets. On a bad day the step gets smaller, it doesn't get skipped.

The room is a mirror

Seeing your own progress is one of the best-supported ways to change behaviour, especially when the record is physical and visible (Harkin et al., 2016, 138 studies). The mess is capped, cute, and never takes away anything you earned.

Missing a day is not a reset

In Lally's study one missed day did not set habits back, so there is no streak to break. After a gap the app says welcome back and suggests the smallest version (Breines & Chen, 2012: self-compassion after a lapse raises motivation to try again).

The routines

Every routine has three sizes of the same act, the mechanism in plain words, where the evidence comes from, one change to your surroundings that makes it easier, and moments it can hang on.
