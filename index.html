02

Screen by screen

Four surfaces. Every one of them is the same component tree as the live build above — these frames are pinned to a state, not redrawn as pictures.

Baseline Calibration

Onboarding · 4 cards · ~40s

No text inputs, no account wall, no value-prop carousel. Four rapid-fire cards, each one question, each answerable with a thumb. The only job is finding where Level 1 sits for this specific person.

  • Layout

    Fixed three-zone column: segment bar pinned top (progress is the only chrome), question + control vertically centred in the free space, advance button pinned bottom at thumb height.

  • Hierarchy

    Question at 26px/-.04em is the loudest thing on screen. The control sits second. The hint sits at 12.5px in --dim and can be skipped without loss.

  • Controls

    Two types only — multi-select chips in a 2-up grid, and a single slider with a live oversized numeric readout in the pillar's accent. The number is the feedback; there is no confirmation state.

  • Accent logic

    Each card adopts its pillar's accent via --pill, so the user learns blue/green/crimson as a vocabulary before they ever reach the dashboard.

  • Exit

    Card 5 is not a question — it's the calibration result. It states the three starting tiers plainly and hands over with one button: BEGIN DAY 001. No tour, no permissions prompt.

  • Friction

    Back is available on cards 2–4 but rendered as a ghost. Nothing is required; skipping a card takes the conservative midpoint and says so.

The Expanding Reality

Dashboard · home · tab 1

Split hard down the middle. Top half is the Bubble Map and belongs to feeling; bottom half is the day's three offers and belongs to deciding. Nothing else competes.

  • Bubble Map

    Canvas, 300px tall, three additively-blended spheres on a faint polar grid. Radius is bound to tier; overlaps light up where pillars reinforce each other. It drifts continuously so the screen is never dead.

  • HUD

    Overlaid, non-interactive: DAY 001 top-left, per-pillar radius legend bottom-left. The map itself has no controls — resisting the urge to make it a toy is what keeps it feeling like an instrument.

  • The offer

    Exactly three cards, one per pillar, in fixed order Access → Activity → Social so muscle memory forms. Each carries pillar name, tier code, the directive, and its three cost tags.

  • Card anatomy

    2px left border in the pillar accent is the only chrome that identifies it. Tap anywhere on the card to activate — no separate CTA button, the card is the button. Hover/press nudges 3px right toward the action.

  • Completed state

    Card drops to 42% opacity with a struck directive and goes pointer-inert. It stays on screen: the day's record should show what was taken as well as what remains.

  • Nav

    Three tabs, bottom-fixed, 66px. Today / Map / Profile. No hamburger, no settings gear on the home surface.

The Action Screen

Challenge · locked focus

One challenge, full bleed, nothing else reachable. The tab bar is gone. This screen has two exits: complete it, or log the friction.

  • Focus

    Tab bar unmounts on entry. The only way back is an explicit ghost ← ABORT, deliberately colder than a chevron.

  • Directive

    26px/800/-.045em, top-left aligned, max four lines. Written as an instruction, never a suggestion — "Step outside your front door", not "Try stepping outside".

  • Parameters

    Three-cell strip: TIME · COST · EXPOSURE. Exposure is the vulnerability rating, 1–10, and it is the number that climbs hardest across 100 tiers.

  • Coach block

    Tinted in the pillar accent. One or two sentences of framing — why this specific tier exists. This is where the voice lives.

  • Commit

    Long-press, 900ms, 104px target, bottom-centred. A stroked ring fills clockwise; releasing early snaps it back to zero. Completion must cost a deliberate second — a tap is too cheap to mean anything.

  • Haptics

    vibrate([6,26,10,40,18]) — a rising pattern fired at ring close, so the phone confirms before the eye does.

  • Second exit

    TOO MUCH TODAY in ember, directly under the commit ring. Not hidden, not shamed. It logs a Friction point and returns to the dashboard.

Completion & Micro-Journal

Reward · one sentence

The reward loop. No confetti — four concentric geometric frames expanding outward from the press point, rotating 45°, easing on cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1) and fading at the edge. The bubble behind it visibly grows by the delta.

  • Sequence

    Ring closes → haptic → burst (4 frames, staggered 120ms) → headline sets → radius delta counts up → journal field slides in from below. Whole beat lands in ~1.4s.

  • Headline

    PERIMETER EXTENDED at 31px, with the measured delta beneath in the pillar accent: +0.4 KM ACCESS RADIUS. Progress is stated as a physical measurement, never as points.

  • Journal

    Single-line input, hard cap 90 characters, live counter. One sentence maximum is a design constraint, not a suggestion — the field physically cannot hold a paragraph.

  • Prompt

    Placeholder reads "What actually happened?" — outcome, not feeling. Feelings are inferred from the Friction log; the journal captures fact.

  • Skip

    Journal is optional and the skip is a plain ghost. Blocking the reward behind writing would make people stop completing challenges.

  • Return

    Lands back on the dashboard with the completed card already struck and the map already grown — the user sees the consequence, not a summary of it.

Profile & the Anti-Score

Friction · streaks · history

The screen that decides whether the product actually works. Streaks reward compliance; Friction rewards contact with the edge. Friction is placed above the streak, in the largest numeral on the surface, so the hierarchy states the thesis without a word of copy.

  • Friction Score

    Ember card, 47px numeral, top of scroll. Increments on three events: a challenge abandoned as too hard, a social attempt refused, an outcome logged as awkward. Nothing here can go down.

  • Reframe

    Caption reads: "27 points of contact with the edge. A zero here means the tiers are too easy." A courage badge tier sits in the corner as a pill.

  • Ember rule

    Ember is used only for Friction, anywhere in the app. It never appears as a warning or an error, so the colour permanently reads as credit rather than fault.

  • Stats

    2-up tiles — day streak, tiers cleared — kept deliberately smaller than the Friction card. Compliance is real but secondary.

  • Per-pillar bars

    Three thin progress bars to 100, each in its accent. Shows imbalance honestly; a user 40 tiers deep in Access and 3 deep in Social should feel that gap here.

  • History log

    Reverse chronological rows: dot, directive, journal line, day index. Friction entries render their index in ember and sit in the same stream as completions — never a separate "failures" list.

04

The escalation ladder

100 tiers per pillar. Sampled here at the inflection points — where the challenge changes category rather than just degree. Between samples the engine interpolates: same action shape, one fractional turn harder.

Rule 01

Micro-beginnings. Tier 1 costs zero money, zero planning, and under two minutes. Failure has to be nearly impossible or the user never reaches tier 5.

Rule 02

Invisible escalation. Difficulty rises on (t/100)^1.9 — the first 30 tiers are almost flat. The user is 40 days in before the curve is felt.

Rule 03

Macro-endings. Tier 100 is geographic displacement, public exposure, or a conversation that changes a relationship. It is supposed to be frightening from tier 1.

Rule 04

Calibration. Onboarding sets the entry index per pillar, so a cyclist with a 40km radius never gets served "walk to the end of your street".

Calibration → entry tier

Access 
t₀ = clamp(1
   + floor(radius_km / 6)
   + transport_modes
   , 1, 14)
Owning a car and a bike and having ranged 30km last month enters at tier 8, not tier 1.
Activity 
t₀ = clamp(1
   + floor((100 − routine) / 11)
   + novelty_recency
   , 1, 14)
Routine is the % of this week identical to last. Novelty recency scores 0–4 by how recently they last tried something new.
Social 
t₀ = clamp(1
   + floor((10 − anxiety) * 0.8)
   + floor(long_convos / 4)
   , 1, 14)
Anxiety is self-reported 0–10. High anxiety plus zero long conversations pins entry at tier 1 — the eye-contact tier.
Runtime adjustment 
on friction  → hold tier, re-serve variant
on 2× friction → step back one tier
on 3× clean   → allow +2 tier jump
The ladder is not a rail. Repeated friction at a tier drops the user down rather than stalling them; three clean completions unlock a double step so fast movers aren't held back.
Daily serve 
serve(pillar) = ladder[pillar][ tier ]
skipped days do not advance tier
max 3 active challenges per day
Tier is bound to completions, not to the calendar. Day 100 of the app is not day 100 of the ladder unless it was earned.

Rules of the surface

01

One accent per screen region. Never two pillars in one card.

02

Ember is Friction. Nothing else, ever.

03

Progress is stated in physical units — km, people, minutes — never points.

04

The map is never interactive. It reports; it isn't a toy.

05

Nothing celebratory is auto-shared. No streak-loss notifications.

06

Every destructive or committing action costs a deliberate 900ms.
